Problem

The Business Problem

85% of users bought tickets once or twice a year and never came back. Premium membership sat at 3.2% conversion with 42% first-year churn. The instinct was to push harder on promotions. The data suggested something else entirely.

85%
Low purchase frequency
The vast majority of users never exceeded 1–2 purchases per year, leaving significant lifetime value on the table.

Premium conversion
Premium membership was underperforming despite offering clear value — the messaging wasn't reaching the right people at the right moment.
42%
First-year premium churn
Nearly half of new premium members cancelled within their first year, suggesting conversion was happening before real value was demonstrated.
4
User segments with distinct behaviors
One-Timers (55%), Casual Buyers (30%), Enthusiasts (7%), and Premium members (8%) each needed a different approach.
02
Strategy

Two insights that changed the approach

Insight 01
Group purchases drove disproportionate value
60% of tickets were bought in groups of two or more. Group buyers paid 3–4x more in fees per transaction, and their Premium ROI was 5x higher than solo buyers. The opportunity wasn't to sell harder — it was to show the math at the right moment.
Insight 02
Users had price comfort zones — and occasionally broke them
40% of users sometimes bought outside their typical price range. Standard recommendation engines missed this. A bidirectional approach — showing both premium and more accessible options — outperformed single-tier messaging by a significant margin.
